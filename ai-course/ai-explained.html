Module 1

What AI is, and where the money goes

AI is one type of software with two jobs, training and inference, and seven layers of companies share the money.

In this module you learn six basic terms, the two jobs of a model, and the seven layers of the AI supply chain.

Six terms that fit inside each other

AI is the widest term, and each of the next four terms is a smaller part of the term before it.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is a machine system that uses its input to make outputs such as predictions, content or decisions. The OECD adopted this definition on 8 November 2023 (OECD, old source, T9-S2).

Machine learning is the part of AI where a computer system learns from data (NIST glossary, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S1). A person does not write each rule.

Deep learning is machine learning with a neural network that has many layers (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S3). A neural network is a model made of layers of simple units, and each unit sends a number to the next layer (Google glossary, read 26 November 2026, T19-S1).

Generative AI is the class of AI models that make new content, such as text, images, sound and code (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024, old source, T9-S3).

A large language model (LLM) is a deep learning model that trains on a very large quantity of text (NIST glossary, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S2). ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are products that use an LLM. An LLM reads and writes tokens. A token is a small piece of text, and module 2 explains it.

An agent is a system where an LLM directs its own steps and uses tools to do a task (Anthropic, 19 December 2024, old source, T9B-S4). A tool is a function of normal software, for example a search or a calculation.

Simplified: the sources do not agree on the position of generative AI. Some sources put it fully inside deep learning. One source says that a small part uses classic machine learning (IBM and Cornell, read 26 November 2026, T14-S17). No standards body publishes this full map.

Worked example

Put one model on the map. Meta's Llama 3.1 405B model has 126 layers (Meta, July 2024, old source, T14-S5). IBM says that a network with more than three layers is deep learning (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T20-S13).

Thus this model is deep learning. It makes text, thus it is also generative AI and an LLM. It becomes part of an agent only when an application gives it tools and a loop.

Check A bank uses a model that reads loan data and gives a risk score. Is this generative AI?

No. The model predicts a number and does not make new content. It is machine learning, and it can also be deep learning.

A model is a very large function

A model is a mathematical function with billions of numbers, and these numbers have the name parameters.

A model is a part of a computer system that uses mathematics to make outputs from given inputs (NIST SP 800-218A, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S1). Numbers go in, the function does its calculation, and numbers come out.

A parameter is one number inside the model that the model learns (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T20-S1). Most parameters are weights. A weight shows how much importance the model gives to one input.

The size of a model is its number of parameters. DeepSeek V4-Pro has 1.6 trillion parameters (DeepSeek, 24 April 2026, T1-S8). Anthropic, OpenAI and Google do not publish the parameter counts of their largest models (research note, estimate, November 2026, T1-S3).

Worked example

Each parameter needs memory. Llama 3.1 405B has 405 billion parameters, and each parameter uses 2 bytes in 16-bit form.

405 billion × 2 bytes = 810 GB. One server with 8 H100 chips has 640 GB of chip memory. Thus the full model does not fit on one such server (model card, estimate, 2024, old source, T9-S19).

Check A model has 70 billion parameters in 16-bit form. How much memory do the parameters need?

About 140 GB. Multiply 70 billion parameters by 2 bytes for each parameter.

Training and inference

Training sets the parameters one time, and inference uses the parameters for each request.

Training is the process that adjusts the parameters of a model with data. Inference is the use of the trained model on new input (Nvidia, October 2025, old source, T3B-S3).

The two jobs have different cost shapes. Training costs a large sum before the model earns revenue. The cost of inference increases with each request.

Three more terms help here. A GPU (graphics processing unit) is a chip that does many calculations at the same time. Compute is the quantity of calculation work that chips do. A GPU-hour is the use of one GPU for one hour.

Training is large work. Epoch AI estimates that the compute for the largest training runs increased about 5 times each year since 2020 (Epoch AI, estimate, February 2026, T9-S16).

Training is also not the full cost of a model. One estimate from Anthropic's filing for its stock market listing puts a maximum of 26 per cent of its compute on paid customer work. The other 74 per cent went to training, research, internal use and free users (chart built from the filing, estimate, November 2026, T6-S17).

Worked example

DeepSeek gave the cost of the final training run of its V3 model. The run used 2.788 million GPU-hours, and DeepSeek assumed a rental price of $2 for each GPU-hour.

2.788 million GPU-hours × $2 = $5.576 million (DeepSeek, December 2024, old source, T9-S40). This sum is the final run only. Epoch AI says that final runs are the smaller part of the compute that a model company uses for research (Epoch AI, estimate, 23 March 2026, T6-S18).

Check A company says that its model cost $6 million to train. Which question do you ask?

Ask what the figure includes. A figure for the final run does not include the earlier research, the failed runs and the staff.

Money enters at the user and goes down through seven layers to the energy supplier.

Each layer sells to the layer above it. Four terms are necessary before you read the table.

  • A cloud is a company that rents computers and services to customers through the internet.
  • A hyperscaler is a very large cloud company, such as Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure or Google Cloud (IBM and Oracle, read 26 November 2026, T14-S16).
  • A data centre is a building that holds computers and gives them power and cooling.
  • Run-rate revenue is the revenue of a short recent period, multiplied to one year. It is not audited revenue.

Which layers earn a profit today

Today the chip layer keeps the largest share of each dollar, and the model layer spends more than it earns.

The chart compares the operating margin of nine companies. Operating margin is operating profit divided by revenue.

Read the chart with three cautions.

  • The clouds earn good margins, but they also pay for the largest capital spending. Capital spending is money that a company spends on assets with a long life, such as chips and buildings.
  • A GPU cloud is a cloud company that rents mainly GPUs. Its margin is thin after the cost of the chips.
  • One quarter does not show the full picture of a model company. Anthropic had positive operating cash flow of $6,569 million in the nine months to 30 September 2026 (filing summary, estimate, November 2026, T6-S4).

Worked example

Compare the revenue from AI with the money that the build costs. One count from company filings puts the AI revenue from companies at $63 billion to $73 billion in May 2026. The hyperscalers planned about $690 billion of capital spending for 2026 (filings count, estimate, 2026, T11B-S9).

$63 billion ÷ $690 billion = 9 per cent. $73 billion ÷ $690 billion = 11 per cent. Thus this revenue covers about one tenth of the capital spending of one year.

Other counts have a different scope. One count puts the revenue of all generative AI companies above $175 billion a year (Exponential View, estimate, 2026, T11B-S9). Use the range, and state the scope.

Check Which layer has the largest margins today, and which layer has operating losses?

The chips layer has the largest margins. The model layer has operating losses. The open question is whether profit moves to other layers in future years.

Investor view

Profit sits low in the stack today, and the revenue at the top is still small against the build cost.

  • Value. Chip, memory and foundry companies keep operating margins of 59 to 80 per cent. Model companies and GPU clouds spend heavily (analysis, estimate, 2026, T11-S17).
  • Risk. Bain said that AI needs $2 trillion of revenue each year by 2030, and it saw a gap of $800 billion (Bain, estimate, 23 September 2025, old source, T15-S20). Other analysts dispute the method (Sequoia and Bain, estimates, T5B-S9).
  • Terms. The words hyperscaler and AI data centre have no legal definition. Check the meaning in each document before you compare figures.

Questions to ask

  • In which layer does this company sit, and which layers does it pay?
  • Does the company buy access to a model, rent GPUs or own chips?
  • What does a quoted training cost include?
  • Is the revenue figure audited revenue or run-rate revenue?

Builder view

An engineer gets a model in one of three ways, and each way changes cost, staff and control.

  • Call a model API. An API (application programming interface) is a way for one piece of software to talk to another (AWS, read 26 November 2026, T20-S3). The team pays for each token and runs no chips.
  • Rent GPUs. The team runs a model that it can download. One analysis puts a model of 70 billion parameters at 4 to 8 rented H100 GPUs. That costs $15,000 to $40,000 each month before staff (analysis, estimate, April 2026, T14-S18).
  • Own chips. Only the largest companies do this, because it needs capital and a data centre.

What fails in practice. No single break-even point exists between an API and rented GPUs. The result depends on the number of requests, the hours of use, the staff cost and the model (analysis, estimate, September 2026, T14-S18). Teams often forget the staff who must run and monitor the model.

Module 2

How a language model works

A language model predicts the next token from probabilities, and it repeats this step until the answer is complete.

In this module you learn how a model changes a prompt into an answer, and why the answer can be false.

Tokens

A model reads and writes tokens, and one token is about three quarters of an English word.

A language model does not read letters or full words. It reads tokens, and a token is a small piece of text. In English, 1 token has about 4 characters, which is about three quarters of a word (OpenAI help centre, read 26 November 2026, T9-S4).

A tokenizer is the tool that splits text into tokens (Google Research, read 26 November 2026, T19-S3). Each model family has its own tokenizer. Thus the same text gives a different token count on a different model.

The count also depends on the language. One study found that the same text can need up to 15 times more tokens in a different language (Petrov and others, estimate, 2023, old source, T9-S8).

Check A contract has 30,000 words. About how many tokens is it with the rule of three quarters?

About 40,000 tokens. Divide 30,000 words by 0.75.

From prompt to answer in five steps

The model gives a probability to each possible next token, chooses one token, and starts again.

A prompt is the text that a user or an application sends to the model. Text goes in, and text comes out. Inside the model, five steps run in a loop.

At step 4 the model gives one raw score to each token in its vocabulary. The vocabulary is the list of all tokens that the model knows. One large model has a vocabulary of 128,256 tokens (Meta, July 2024, old source, T14-S5).

A function with the name softmax changes the raw scores into probabilities that add up to 1. At step 5 the model does not always take the most probable token. Sampling draws a token at random in proportion to its probability. Thus the same prompt can give different answers (tutorial, read 26 November 2026, T14-S1).

Simplified: no single source lists all the steps in this order. The course put the chain together from several sources (research note, uncertain, 2026, T14-S1). A real model also stores results from earlier tokens to save work.

Worked example

The model gives three tokens the raw scores 2.0, 1.0 and 0.1. Softmax raises the number e to the power of each score. The results are 7.39, 2.72 and 1.11, and their sum is 11.21.

Divide each result by the sum. The probabilities are 66 per cent, 24 per cent and 10 per cent. The model chooses the first token in about two of three runs.

Check Explain in two sentences how a language model makes an answer.

The model splits the prompt into tokens and calculates a probability for each possible next token. It chooses one token, adds it to the text, and repeats until the answer is complete.

Embeddings

An embedding is a list of numbers that holds the meaning of a token or a text.

An embedding is a list of numbers that stands for the meaning of a piece of text. Texts with a similar meaning get similar lists (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T14-S3).

You can compare an embedding to a position on a map. Words with a related meaning are near each other on the map.

Worked example

The small embedding model of OpenAI returns 1,536 numbers for each text. It costs $0.02 for each million tokens (OpenAI, read 26 November 2026, T9-S12).

A company has documents with 10 million tokens in total. The cost to make the embeddings is 10 × $0.02 = $0.20. Thus the embeddings are a very small cost. The answers of the model are the large cost.

Check A user types "bill", and the search finds a document with the word "invoice". Why?

The two words have similar meanings, thus their embeddings are near each other. The search compares the positions of the embeddings.

Attention

Attention lets the model weigh each token against all the other tokens in the context.

Attention is the mechanism that weighs how important each token is to each other token (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T14-S4). With attention, the model can connect words that are far apart in the text.

A paper of 12 June 2017 proposed a model built only on attention (Vaswani and others, 2017, old source, T9-S11). This design has the name transformer, and the LLMs of today use it.

Attention has a cost. The model compares each token with each other token. Thus the work increases with the square of the length of the input (course notes, read 26 November 2026, T14-S6).

Worked example

An input of 1,000 tokens needs 1,000 × 1,000 = 1 million comparisons. An input of 10,000 tokens needs 10,000 × 10,000 = 100 million comparisons.

Thus 10 times more text needs about 100 times more comparisons. This is one reason why a long prompt costs more to serve.

Check A prompt increases from 2,000 tokens to 6,000 tokens. By how much does the number of comparisons increase?

By about 9 times. The length is 3 times larger, and 3 × 3 = 9.

Check A supplier says that its model has more parameters than a rival model. Is this proof of better quality?

No. The method of training also decides quality. Ask for results on your own task.

The three stages of training

A model learns language from very large text, then a second round adapts it, then feedback improves its behaviour.

Fine-tuning is a second round of training that adapts a trained model to a specific task (Google glossary, read 26 November 2026, T19-S4). In stage 3, a common method uses ratings from people to guide the training (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T19-S5).

Fine-tuning and retrieval do different jobs. Retrieval means that the application finds relevant documents and puts them in the prompt. Module 8 explains it.

  • Use retrieval when the model lacks facts, such as new data or private data.
  • Use fine-tuning when the model lacks a behaviour, such as a style or a format (teaching sources, estimate, 2026, T14-S14).

OpenAI now closes fine-tuning for most customers. A customer that did not use it before 7 May 2026 cannot start. Active customers cannot start new jobs after 6 January 2027 (OpenAI, 2026, T14-S11).

Worked example

DeepMind trained a model of 70 billion parameters on 1.4 trillion tokens (DeepMind, March 2022, old source, T9-S14). 1.4 trillion ÷ 70 billion = 20.

Thus the training used about 20 tokens of text for each parameter. This small model was better than an older model with 280 billion parameters.

Check A law firm wants answers that use its own new documents. Is fine-tuning the first method to try?

No. The model lacks facts, thus retrieval is the first method. Fine-tuning changes behaviour, and it adds facts poorly.

Temperature

Temperature changes how often the model chooses a less probable token.

Temperature is a setting that divides the raw scores before softmax. A low value makes the top token more probable. A high value makes the probabilities more equal (tutorial, read 26 November 2026, T14-S1).

Worked example

Use the scores 2.0, 1.0 and 0.1 again, with a temperature of 0.5. Divide each score by 0.5. The new scores are 4.0, 2.0 and 0.2.

Softmax gives 54.60, 7.39 and 1.22, with a sum of 63.21. The probabilities are 86 per cent, 12 per cent and 2 per cent.

Many of the newest models do not accept this setting. Anthropic says that developers must omit temperature on Claude 4.7 and later models, and a value returns an error (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T20-S14). Google recommends the default of 1.0 for all Gemini 3 models (Google, read 26 November 2026, T20-S16).

Check You want the same answer each time. Does a temperature of 0 guarantee it?

No. Anthropic says that the output is not fully fixed even at 0.0 (Anthropic API reference, read 26 November 2026, T14-S9). Test with repeated runs.

Why a model can state false things

A model chooses probable tokens and does not look up facts, thus a fluent answer can be false.

A hallucination is an output that is false but looks correct. The model samples from probabilities, thus a wrong but plausible answer is always possible.

OpenAI researchers give a second cause. Training and tests reward a guess more than the answer "I do not know" (OpenAI, 5 September 2025, old source, T9-S32).

The three models in the summary test are GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon. Their rates are 8.7, 9.9 and 12.1 per cent. The lowest rate on that list is 1.8 per cent, from a small model (Vectara, 24 November 2026, T9B-S15).

Methods decrease the rate, but they do not remove it. Anthropic lists three methods (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T9-S33).

  • Let the model say that it does not know.
  • Ask the model to find exact quotes first.
  • Ask for a quote that supports each claim.

Worked example

A legal research tool answers 200 questions each day. Use the lower Stanford rate of 17 per cent. 200 × 0.17 = 34.

Thus about 34 answers each day contain a false statement. A lawyer must check each answer, and that check has a cost.

Check A supplier says that its model hallucinates only 2 per cent of the time. Which two questions do you ask?

Ask which test gave the figure. Ask how the test counts a refusal to answer. On one test, a model that refuses more often gets a better score (Artificial Analysis, 2025, old source, T1-S21).

The context window

The context window is the fixed space for all the text that the model can read at one time.

The context window is the text that the model can read at one time. You can compare the context window to a desk with a fixed size. When the desk is full, you must remove old papers before you add new papers.

The context is the text that is in the window for one request. The model has no other memory of the conversation.

The newest large models have a window of about 1 million tokens. Recent Claude models have 1,000,000 tokens and can write up to 128,000 output tokens (Anthropic, 2026, T1-S3). GPT-6.2 Astra has 1,050,000 tokens (OpenAI, November 2026, T1-S1).

A large window is not the same as good recall. One study found that models answer worse when key facts are in the middle of a long input (Liu and others, 2023, old source, T9-S27). Chroma tested 18 models and found that accuracy becomes less reliable as the input gets longer (Chroma, 14 July 2025, old source, T9-S28).

A long context also costs more, because the supplier bills each input token for each request. Anthropic bills the full request at a higher rate when the input is more than 200,000 tokens (Anthropic, November 2026, T2-S2).

Worked example

A window has 1,000,000 tokens. With Anthropic's figure of 555,000 words for each million tokens, the window holds 555,000 words.

Assume a long book of 100,000 words. This is an example value. 555,000 ÷ 100,000 = 5.5. Thus the window holds about five such books at one time.

Check A long conversation fills the context window. What must the application do before the next message?

It must remove old text, or replace old text with a short summary. The model cannot read text that is not in the window.

The system prompt and the cut-off date

The application writes the system prompt, and the model knows nothing after its cut-off date.

The system prompt is the set of instructions that the application gives to the model before the question of the user. It sets the role, the tone and the rules (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S3).

The knowledge cut-off is the date up to which the training information of a model is reliable (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S4). The model does not know later events.

A search tool decreases this problem. But then the quality of the answer depends on the search step and on its sources.

Worked example

Gemini 4 Argon has a cut-off of March 2026 (Google, 2026, T9B-S10). The date of this course is 26 November 2026.

Thus the model does not know the events of the last 8 months. The application must supply them through a search tool or through documents.

Check Who writes the system prompt: the user, the application or the model company?

The application writes it. The user usually does not see it. It is one of the places where a product team adds its own knowledge.

Reasoning tokens

A reasoning model writes working text before its answer, and the supplier bills this text as output.

Reasoning tokens are tokens that the model writes as working text before the final answer. The user usually does not see them. OpenAI and Anthropic bill them at the output price (OpenAI, read 26 November 2026, T9-S30) (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T9-S31).

Reasoning helps on hard problems. On one mathematics test, an OpenAI model without reasoning solved 12 per cent of the problems. The reasoning model o1 solved 74 per cent (OpenAI, 12 September 2024, old source, T9B-S11).

An effort setting lets the developer choose how much the model reasons. Anthropic says that the highest setting often adds cost for a small gain (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S14).

Worked example

One measurement of Claude Opus 5.5 shows the time to the first token of the answer. It is 6.16 seconds at low effort and 154.80 seconds at very high effort (Artificial Analysis, estimate, November 2026, T2B-S13).

154.80 ÷ 6.16 = 25. Thus the user waits about 25 times longer for the first word at very high effort.

Check The visible answer has 300 tokens, but the bill shows 2,300 output tokens. What is the probable cause?

The model wrote about 2,000 reasoning tokens. The supplier bills them as output, although the user does not see them.

Images, sound and video

Some models also read images, sound and video, and the supplier counts these inputs in tokens.

A multimodal model is a model that accepts more than one type of input. The three newest large models are different on this point.

Open-weight models and closed models

An open-weight model publishes its parameters, and a closed model gives access only through its supplier.

An open-weight model is a model whose trained parameters anyone can download. The supplier often does not publish the training data or the training code (Open Source Initiative, October 2024, old source, T9-S38).

A closed model is a model that customers can use only through the products or the API of its supplier. The largest models of Anthropic, OpenAI and Google are closed. Meta moved to a closed model in April 2026 (Meta, April 2026, T1-S7).

  • Quality. Epoch AI estimates that open-weight models are about four months behind the best closed models (Epoch AI, estimate, 29 May 2026, T1-S18).
  • Share. On OpenRouter, open-weight models stayed above 62 per cent of tokens. But they had only about one quarter of paid spend in October 2026 (OpenRouter, October 2026, T1-S19).
  • Licence. Read each licence. The DeepSeek-V3 licence bans military use, and Chinese law governs it (DeepSeek licence, read 26 November 2026, T9B-S22).

OpenRouter is a service for developers who look for low prices. Thus its share is not the share of all company use.

Worked example

Can one GPU with 80 GB of memory hold the open-weight model gpt-oss-120b? The model has 117 billion parameters, and it stores most of them in 4 bits (OpenAI, August 2025, old source, T9-S39).

A rule of thumb is parameters × bits ÷ 8, plus about 20 per cent (guide, estimate, 2026, T9B-S20). 117 billion × 4 ÷ 8 = 58.5 GB. With 20 per cent more, the result is about 70 GB. Thus the model fits.

No. The company must pay for GPUs and for staff to run the model. It must also obey the licence of the model.

Benchmarks

A benchmark gives a model a score on a fixed set of tasks, and the score can mislead for four reasons.

A benchmark is a standard set of tasks that gives a score to a model (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T20-S5). The methods are different. One benchmark uses votes from people. A second uses graded tests. A third measures the length of the tasks that an agent completes (guide, estimate, 2026, T13-S24).

  1. The test leaks into the training data. OpenAI stopped its reports of one coding benchmark in February 2026 for this reason (OpenAI, February 2026, T1-S20).
  2. The test becomes too easy. The top score on the ARC-AGI-2 test reached 95.0 per cent, thus the test no longer separates the best models (leaderboard summaries, estimate, September 2026, T1-S13).
  3. A supplier can tune a model for the test. In April 2025 Meta sent an experimental chat version of a model to a public ranking. It ranked second. The public version ranked below three older rival models (TechCrunch, April 2025, old source, T1B-S11).
  4. The result has a wide error range. The chart below shows one example. A second search did not confirm the hours for the two newest models, so treat them as uncertain.

IBM says that a benchmark cannot predict how a model works in real use (IBM, read 26 November 2026, T20-S5). Thus engineers build a test on their own task. Such a test has the name evaluation, and module 8 explains it.

Worked example

The central value for GPT-6 Astra is 40 hours, and the upper limit of the range is 2,645 hours. 2,645 ÷ 40 = 66.

Thus the upper limit is 66 times the central value. Do not use the central value alone in a forecast.

Give two of these four reasons. The test leaked into the training data. The test became too easy. The supplier tuned the model for the test. The result has a wide error range.

Investor view

The mechanism explains the margin: output tokens cost the most, and a false answer is always possible.

  • Value. The model writes output one token at a time, thus output costs more than input. A product that writes long answers has weaker margins than a product that mostly reads.
  • Risk. Hallucination limits use in law, medicine and finance, where one false fact is costly. Retrieval and quotes decrease the rate but do not remove it.
  • Price pressure. Open-weight models put a limit on the price of common tasks. But a company needs GPUs and skills to run them.
  • Speed of progress. METR reports that the task length doubles in about 3.6 months, with a range of 2.08 to 4.94 months (METR, 20 November 2026, T1-S11). Critics point to the very wide error ranges.

Why output costs more than input

The model reads all input tokens in one pass, but it writes each output token in its own pass.

Nvidia splits the work of a model into two phases. In the first phase the model reads all input tokens at one time. In the second phase it writes the output tokens one at a time (Nvidia, read 26 November 2026, T14-S7).

On the price list above, output costs 3 to 6 times the input price. The sources do not agree on the reason for the size of this gap.

  • One analysis says that the costs justify an input price of about one third of the output price (DigitalOcean, estimate, 2025, old source, T9-S23).
  • One discussion argues that the raw compute is similar, and that memory needs and a price decision explain the gap (LessWrong, estimate, 2026, T2B-S15).

Worked example

Use the question from the first example on GPT-6.2 Sol. The input has 2,000 tokens and costs $0.004. The output has 500 tokens and costs $0.005.

$0.005 ÷ $0.009 = 56 per cent. Thus the answer is one fifth of the tokens, but it is more than half of the cost.

Product B. It costs about $0.10 for each request, and product A costs about $0.02. Output is the expensive part.

Reasoning tokens on the bill

Reasoning tokens are output tokens that the user does not see, and they can multiply the cost of a task.

Module 2 showed that a reasoning model writes working text before its answer. The supplier bills this text at the output price. Thus the cost of a task can change many times for the same prompt.

Worked example

The visible answer has 500 tokens. The model also writes 2,000 reasoning tokens. This number is an example value. The output price is $10.

The output cost is 2,500 × $10 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.025. Without reasoning it was $0.005. With the input of $0.004, the request now costs $0.029 and not $0.009.

Check How can a developer limit the cost of reasoning tokens?

The developer sets a lower reasoning effort for routine requests, and sets a maximum number of output tokens.

The cache and the batch discount

Two discounts decrease the bill: a cache for text that repeats, and a batch for work that can wait.

Prompt caching stores the start of a prompt. Later requests use the stored text again at a low price (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T19-S10). A long system prompt that each request repeats is a good example.

  • At Anthropic, a cache read costs 0.1 times the input price or less. A cache write for 5 minutes costs 1.25 times the input price (Anthropic, November 2026, T2-S2).
  • At OpenAI, cached input on GPT-6.2 Sol costs $0.10 against $2.00 for normal input (OpenAI rate card, November 2026, T2-S3).

A batch request is a job of many requests that the supplier answers later. Anthropic and OpenAI give 50 per cent off for batch work, and OpenAI completes a batch within 24 hours (Anthropic, November 2026, T2-S2) (OpenAI, November 2026, T2-S11).

The real saving from a cache is smaller than the headline number. One analysis says that a 90 per cent cut on cached input gives only a 25 to 35 per cent cut in total cost (analysis, estimate, 2026, T2B-S5).

Worked example

A request has 10,000 input tokens and 500 output tokens on GPT-6.2 Sol. Without a cache, the cost is $0.020 + $0.005 = $0.025.

Now 90 per cent of the input comes from the cache. Normal input: 1,000 × $2 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.002. Cached input: 9,000 × $0.10 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.0009. Output: $0.005. Total: $0.0079.

The cache decreases the total cost by 68 per cent in this example. The saving is large because the input is 20 times the output.

Check Why does a cache help little when the prompt is short and the answer is long?

The cache gives a discount only on input tokens. In that request the output tokens are almost all of the cost.

Long conversations and agents

The model has no memory, thus each new turn sends the full conversation again, and an agent does the same at each step.

A conversation has turns. At each turn the application sends all earlier messages again, and the supplier bills them again as input (OpenAI, 2026, T10-S28). Thus each turn costs more than the turn before it.

An agent works in steps. At each step it calls the model, reads the result of a tool and decides the next step. Each call sends the full context. Thus an agent that takes 20 steps sends its context 20 times.

Worked example

An agent takes 10 steps on GPT-6.2 Sol. The context starts at 1,000 tokens. Each step adds 1,000 tokens to the context and writes 200 output tokens.

Input: 1,000 + 2,000 + ... + 10,000 = 55,000 tokens, which cost $0.11. Output: 10 × 200 = 2,000 tokens, which cost $0.02. The task costs $0.13.

Now the agent takes 20 steps. The input is 210,000 tokens and the output is 4,000 tokens. The task costs $0.42 + $0.04 = $0.46. Thus twice the steps cost 3.5 times more.

Measured use agrees with this arithmetic. Gartner found that agents use 5 to 30 times more tokens for each task than a standard chatbot (Gartner, estimate, March 2026, T15-S15).

Anthropic reports an average cost of about $13 for each developer for each active day of its coding agent, Claude Code. That is $150 to $250 for each developer each month (Anthropic, 2026, T2B-S1).

30,000 tokens. Add 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, 8,000 and 10,000.

How fast prices fall, and why spend still rises

The price for a fixed level of quality falls fast, but the price of the best model does not fall, and volume rises faster.

Epoch AI finds that the price for a fixed level of quality falls about 47 per cent each quarter. That is about 13 times each year (Epoch AI, estimate, 2026, T2-S12). Other work from Epoch gives a slower rate of 4.7 times each year. Use a range, and say that the sources do not agree.

The list prices of the best models did not fall. Claude Fable 5.2 costs $8 and $40, which is above Claude Opus 5.5 at $4 and $20. The price of Gemini 4 Argon doubles on 1 December 2026 (price guides, estimate, November 2026, T2-S1).

Total spend can rise while unit prices fall. Three causes work together.

  • Volume. Google processed 1.3 quadrillion tokens each month in October 2025, and more than 3.2 quadrillion in May 2026 (Google, May 2026, T2-S15).
  • More tokens for each task. Reasoning and agents multiply the tokens of one task.
  • Mix. Customers move work to the more expensive models. Microsoft reported higher revenue for each token in October 2026 (call summaries, estimate, October 2026, T2-S17).

Worked example

Google's monthly tokens rose from 1.3 to 3.2 quadrillion in 7 months. 3.2 ÷ 1.3 = 2.5 times.

Assume that the average price falls by half in the same period. This is an example value. The spend changes by 0.5 × 2.5 = 1.25. Thus the spend rises 25 per cent although the price fell 50 per cent.

The fall is for a fixed quality. The price of the best model did not fall, and the tokens for each task increase. Thus the bill can still rise.

Speed and rate limits

Two numbers describe the speed of a model, and a rate limit sets the maximum that a customer can send.

Time to first token is the delay between the request and the first output token (Nvidia glossary, read 26 November 2026, T19-S9). Tokens for each second is the speed of the output after the first token.

The median reasoning model writes about 71 tokens each second (Artificial Analysis, estimate, 2026, T11-S40). Special hosts are much faster. Cerebras serves one open-weight model at about 1,650 to 1,920 tokens each second (Artificial Analysis, estimate, 2026, T2-S18).

A rate limit is a maximum on the quantity that a customer can send to an API in one minute. When the customer exceeds it, the API returns the error 429 and a wait time (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T19-S11).

Suppliers count requests, input tokens and output tokens for each minute. The limits rise with the money that the customer paid. At OpenAI, the first tier needs $5 paid and has a spend limit of $100 each month. The fifth tier needs $1,000 paid (OpenAI, 2026, T2B-S12).

Worked example

One model page of OpenAI lists 500 requests each minute at the first tier (OpenAI, 2026, T18-S2). 500 ÷ 60 = about 8 requests each second.

Thus a new application with a small number of busy users can reach the limit. The fifth tier allows 15,000 requests each minute, which is 250 each second.

Check An application receives the error 429 from a model API. What does it mean, and what must the application do?

The application exceeded its rate limit. It must wait for the time that the API states, and then send the request again.

Investor view

Unit prices fall, but mix and volume decide the bill, thus a lower price does not mean a lower cost.

  • Value. Small models are very cheap, and the prices of the largest models are flat or higher. The revenue of a model company depends on the mix.
  • Risk for the buyer. Hidden reasoning tokens and agent loops increase the tokens for each task. A budget from seat prices is too low for heavy users.
  • Risk for the seller. A fixed seat price with a usage cost can give a negative margin on heavy users.
  • Caution. Token volume is activity, and it is not revenue. Claims about cache savings come from suppliers and customers, and the total saving is often 25 to 60 per cent (customer reports, estimate, 2026, T2B-S5).

  • What happens to your margin when the supplier increases a price?
  • How do you stop an agent that runs too many steps?

Builder view

An engineer uses five methods to decrease token cost, and logs each request to see the effect.

  1. A smaller model for simple work. The application sends easy requests to a cheap model. One research paper reports cost savings of more than 2 times with little loss of quality (Berkeley, estimate, 2024, old source, T2B-S6).
  2. The cache. Put the text that does not change at the start of the prompt. One team raised its cache use from 7 to 74 per cent only with a new order of the prompt (ProjectDiscovery, estimate, 2026, T2B-S5).
  3. Batch requests. Send work that can wait as a batch, for 50 per cent off.
  4. A shorter context. Remove old turns, and use retrieval to send only the relevant text.
  5. A limit on output. Set a maximum number of output tokens and a lower reasoning effort.

Tools. A gateway is a service between the application and the model suppliers. It adds budgets, logs and a change of model. The Vercel AI Gateway adds no charge to the token price (Vercel, November 2026, T2B-S8).

What fails in practice. A request that exceeds a length limit gets a higher price for all its tokens. A new application reaches its rate limit on the day of its launch. A team that does not log the tokens of each request cannot find the cause of a large bill.

Chips and hardware

One type of chip, the GPU, does most AI work, and a short supply chain with few suppliers makes it.

In this module you learn what a GPU does, how GPUs become a cluster, and which companies make the chips.

What a GPU is, and why AI needs it

AI work is one simple calculation repeated billions of times, and a GPU does many of these calculations at the same time.

The work of a neural network is mostly the multiplication of large tables of numbers. A GPU does this work in parallel across thousands of small processors (Nvidia, read 26 November 2026, T3B-S1).

A CPU (central processing unit) is the general chip of a computer. It has fewer and stronger processors (Google Cloud, read 26 November 2026, T3B-S2). You can compare a CPU to a small team of experts, and a GPU to a very large team that does one simple task.

Two more terms are necessary. High-bandwidth memory (HBM) is fast memory that sits in the same package as the GPU and feeds it data (IONOS, estimate, read 26 November 2026, T19-S15). CUDA is the software platform of Nvidia for work on its GPUs (Nvidia, read 26 November 2026, T19-S13).

Worked example

Nvidia had revenue of $115.8 billion in the quarter to 25 October 2026. Its data centre revenue was $107.6 billion (Nvidia, 17 November 2026, T3-S1).

$107.6 billion ÷ $115.8 billion = 93 per cent. Thus almost all of the revenue of the largest GPU company comes from data centres.

Check Why is a GPU better than a CPU for AI work?

AI work is many equal multiplications. A GPU does thousands of them at the same time, and a CPU does a small number.

Chips for training and chips for inference

Training needs raw power and fast links, and inference needs fast memory and a low cost for each token.

Training uses many GPUs together for weeks, thus it needs fast links between the chips. Inference serves many small requests, thus it needs fast memory and a short delay for the user.

Chip designers now give each job its own emphasis. Google calls Ironwood, its seventh AI chip, its first chip built for inference. Google says that the chip also trains large models. Thus the split is an emphasis and not a hard line (Google, April 2025, old source, T3B-S4).

Some companies sell chips only for fast inference. Cerebras expects revenue of $940 million to $960 million for 2026 (secondary summary, November 2026, T3B-S23). Nvidia licensed the inference technology of Groq in December 2025 for a reported sum of about $20 billion (press, estimate, 2025, T3B-S23).

Worked example

Nvidia says that its Vera Rubin rack gives a cost for each token that is up to 10 times lower than its Blackwell rack. The figure of 10 times comes from one model (Nvidia, supplier claim, January 2026, T3B-S16).

A cost for each token of $1.00 then becomes $0.10 on that model. On a different model the gain can be smaller. No independent test of this claim was found.

Ask which model, which length of context and which delay the test used. Then ask for the result on your own workload.

The software is the barrier. AWS says that code written for CUDA needs a rewrite before it runs on Trainium (AWS, 2026, T14-S21). Google and Meta published software in October 2026 that makes the move to TPUs simpler (Google, October 2026, T14-S20).

Worked example

One report says that the image company Midjourney moved its work to TPUs. Its compute cost fell from $2.1 million to $700,000 each month (press, estimate, 2026, T3B-S12).

$700,000 ÷ $2.1 million = 33 per cent. Thus the reported saving is 67 per cent. This is one case from the press, and it is not an independent test.

Check A custom chip is cheaper for each token. Why do most companies still use Nvidia GPUs?

Most AI software uses CUDA, and a move to a different chip needs a rewrite of code. Custom chips are also available only in the cloud of their owner.

Memory and the network inside a cluster

A cluster is as fast as its memory and its network allow, not as fast as its GPUs alone.

A cluster is a group of servers that work as one system. Two parts limit a cluster: the memory beside each GPU and the links between the GPUs.

  • Memory. Epoch AI estimates that HBM was about 63 per cent of the component cost of an AI chip at the end of 2025 (Epoch AI, estimate, 2026, T3B-S19). Three companies make it: SK Hynix with 50 per cent, Samsung with 33 per cent and Micron with 18 per cent (Counterpoint, estimate, second quarter of 2026, T3B-S21).
  • Links inside a rack. NVLink is the fast link of Nvidia between GPUs (Nvidia, read 26 November 2026, T19-S14).
  • Links between racks. Operators use Ethernet or InfiniBand, which are two types of fast network. Ethernet passed InfiniBand in AI clusters in 2025 (Dell'Oro, estimate, 2026, T3B-S17).

Large clusters also fail often. Meta trained one model on 16,384 H100 GPUs for 54 days. The run had 419 stops that nobody planned. GPU faults caused 148 stops, and memory faults caused 72 (Meta, 2024, old source, T3B-S6).

Worked example

In one reference design, NVLink gives each GPU 14.4 Tbps inside the rack. The network card for the links between racks gives 800 Gbps, which is 0.8 Tbps (supplier design, 2024 to 2026, T3B-S18).

14.4 ÷ 0.8 = 18. Thus the link inside the rack is 18 times faster. When a job must cross between racks, the slow link holds the GPUs back.

Check The run of Meta had 419 stops in 54 days. How many stops is that for each day?

About 8 stops each day, which is one stop about every 3 hours. Thus a team must save its work often.

The scale ladder

The same chip repeats at five scales, and the power increases more than 10 times at each step.

A server is one computer in a data centre. A rack is a metal frame that holds servers. A hall is one large room of racks, and a campus is a site with many halls.

The values are for these products. The GPU is the H100. The server is the Nvidia DGX H100. The rack is the GB200 NVL72. The hall is one design of Schneider Electric for such racks. The campus is the site of Anthropic and Amazon in New Carlisle, Indiana.

The next rack generation needs more power. A Vera Rubin NVL72 rack draws 180 to 220 kW in one model (SemiAnalysis, estimate, 2026, T3-S7). The research found no typical size for an AI hall.

Worked example

A GB200 rack holds 72 GPUs and draws about 132 kW. 132 ÷ 72 = 1.8 kW for each GPU.

The GPU itself is rated at about 1.0 kW (press, estimate, 2024, old source, T16-S1). The other 0.8 kW feeds the CPUs, the memory and the network in the rack. Thus 1 MW of rack power serves about 550 GPUs.

Check Air cooling reaches its limit near 30 to 40 kW for each rack. A GB200 rack draws 132 kW. What follows?

The rack needs liquid cooling. An old data centre with only air cooling cannot hold these racks (Jabil and SemiAnalysis, estimate, 2026, T4-S31).

The chip supply chain

Four types of company make one AI chip, and a small number of suppliers control each step.

  • A fabless company designs chips and owns no factory.
  • A foundry is a factory that makes chips to the designs of other companies (glossaries, uncertain, undated, T20-S7).
  • A memory maker produces the HBM.
  • An equipment maker builds the machines that the factories use.

Advanced packaging then joins the processor and its HBM in one package (TSMC, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S11). A wafer is the round slice of silicon on which a factory makes many chips.

Each step is short of capacity. ASML says that its planned supply for 2027 and 2028 is below the demand of its customers (ASML, October 2026, T3-S22). Taiwan makes about 90 per cent of the most advanced chips (estimate, 2022 to 2024, old source, T12B-S22).

Worked example

The packaging capacity of TSMC was about 70,000 wafers each month in 2025. It can reach 125,000 to 140,000 wafers each month at the end of 2026 (TrendForce, estimate, 2026, T3-S21).

125,000 ÷ 70,000 = 1.8, and 140,000 ÷ 70,000 = 2.0. Thus the capacity almost doubles in one year. Morgan Stanley estimates that Nvidia holds about 60 per cent of it.

Check Which company on the map is a foundry, and which company is fabless?

TSMC is the foundry. Nvidia is fabless, because it designs chips and TSMC makes them.

The yearly cycle, and the value of old chips

Nvidia releases a new generation each year, and the sources do not agree on how fast an old chip loses value.

A new generation decreases the value of the old chips. The sources give two different pictures of the speed.

  • Fast loss. A used H100 sells for about $15,000 to $28,000. One dealer forecasts a value of 11 to 19 per cent of the list price in 2027, and 5 to 7 per cent in 2028 (American Compute, estimate, 2026, T3-S23).
  • Slow loss. On one index, the rental price of an H100 rose from $2.00 to $2.88 for each hour between December 2025 and October 2026 (Silicon Data, estimate, October 2026, T3B-S9). Old chips still earn money.

Worked example

A buyer paid $31,000 for one H100 with its share of the server. Apply the forecast of the dealer for 2028, which is 5 to 7 per cent.

$31,000 × 0.05 = $1,550, and $31,000 × 0.07 = $2,170. Thus the chip has almost no sale value in 2028 on this forecast. But it can still earn rent if demand stays high.

No. It shows that demand was high in 2026. The next generations can decrease the rent again. Both views use real data.

Builder view

Module 5

Data centres and energy

An AI data centre is a power project with chips inside, and the wait for power limits growth more than the price of power.

In this module you learn the parts of an AI data centre, its units of power, its cost and the three limits on its growth.

The parts of an AI data centre

A data centre is a chain that brings power to the chips and takes the heat away.

The power goes through six stages before it reaches a chip. Each stage needs a spare unit, because a fault at one stage stops all the stages after it.

  • A substation connects the site to the grid.
  • A power transformer decreases the voltage in steps.
  • Switchgear divides the site into blocks that the operator can switch off separately.
  • A UPS (uninterruptible power supply) is a battery system that carries the load when the grid fails.
  • Generators give backup power for a long period, but they start slowly.

The UPS usually carries the load for 2 to 10 minutes until the generators run (SemiAnalysis and McKinsey, estimate, 2026, T4B-S7).

Worked example

A rack in a traditional data centre draws 5 to 15 kW. An AI rack draws 60 to 120 kW or more (SemiAnalysis and McKinsey, estimate, 2026, T4B-S7).

120 ÷ 15 = 8, and 120 ÷ 5 = 24. Thus one AI rack needs the power of 8 to 24 traditional racks. The electrical parts of the building must be much larger.

Check The grid fails. Which part carries the load in the first minutes?

The UPS carries the load with its batteries. Then the generators start and take the load.

kW, MW and GW

Power is the size of a site, and energy is power multiplied by time.

The watt (W) is the unit of power. People state the size of a data centre in power, and not in floor area.

  • 1 kilowatt (kW) = 1,000 W. One AI server draws about 10 kW.
  • 1 megawatt (MW) = 1,000 kW. One data hall draws some megawatts.
  • 1 gigawatt (GW) = 1,000 MW. The largest campus draws about 1 GW.

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the energy that 1 kW uses in one hour. An electricity bill counts kWh. One terawatt-hour (TWh) is one billion kWh.

Worked example

A site of 1 GW runs at full load for one year. A year has 8,760 hours. 1 GW × 8,760 hours = 8,760 GWh, which is 8.76 TWh.

All data centres in Great Britain used about 4.5 TWh in 2024 (DESNZ, 2026, T4B-S16). Thus one such site uses about two times the energy of all those data centres.

The world totals are estimates. The IEA estimates that data centres used 415 TWh in 2024, which is about 1.5 per cent of world electricity. It projects about 945 TWh in 2030 (IEA, estimate, 2025, T4-S12).

The forecasts for the United States do not agree. Berkeley Lab sees data centres at 11.8 per cent of electricity in 2030, in a range of 9.5 to 15.3 per cent (Berkeley Lab, estimate, 2026, T4-S13). A second group gives 9 to 17 per cent (EPRI, estimate, 2026, T4-S14).

Check A data hall has a power of 7.4 MW. How much energy does it use in one day at full load?

About 178 MWh. Multiply 7.4 MW by 24 hours.

Cooling and PUE

PUE shows how much extra power a site uses for cooling and other overheads.

PUE (power usage effectiveness) is the total power of a data centre divided by the power of its IT equipment. A value near 1.0 means little waste (HPE, read 26 November 2026, T19-S16).

Liquid cooling removes heat with a liquid that flows to the chips. Air cooling reaches its limit near 30 to 40 kW for each rack, thus the newest AI racks need liquid cooling (Jabil and SemiAnalysis, estimate, 2026, T4-S31).

Worked example

A site has an IT load of 100 MW. At a PUE of 1.52, the total power is 100 × 1.52 = 152 MW. At a PUE of 1.09, it is 109 MW.

152 − 109 = 43. Thus the better site saves 43 MW. At the same grid connection, it can run more chips.

Simplified: PUE counts only the overheads of the building. It does not show the efficiency of the chips or the source of the electricity. Operators also draw the measurement boundary in different places.

Check A site draws 60 MW in total, and its IT equipment draws 50 MW. What is its PUE?

1.2. Divide 60 MW by 50 MW.

The time to build

A building takes 1 to 2 years, but a grid connection can take much longer.

Speed is possible where power is available. At the Abilene site in Texas, the builder needs about 10 months for each hall (Crusoe, 2026, T4B-S8). Epoch AI finds 1 to 3.6 years from the start of construction to 1 GW (Epoch AI, estimate, 2026, T4B-S8).

The equipment for power is also slow to get. GE Vernova now sells delivery slots for gas turbines in 2031 (GE Vernova, 2026, T4-S17).

Worked example

A developer in a European hub completes a building in 2 years. The grid connection takes 7 years.

7 − 2 = 5. Thus the finished building waits 5 years for power, unless the developer finds a different source of power.

The turbines give power sooner than the grid. Time has more value than the price of power, because the chips are the large cost.

The three limits on growth

Power, chips and capital limit the build, and each limit has its own delay.

Worked example

The four largest hyperscalers plan $725 billion to $785 billion of capital spending for 2026, by two counts. This example uses the lower count. A typical site of 1 GW costs about $38 billion in the model of Epoch AI.

$725 billion ÷ $38 billion = 19. Thus this spending pays for a maximum of about 19 GW in one year. The real number is lower, because the spending also pays for other things.

Power. The site needs a grid connection or its own power plant, and both can take years.

The largest AI sites of November 2026 draw about 1 GW each.

The cost estimates for these sites are far above the statements of the companies. Amazon announced $11 billion for New Carlisle, and Epoch AI estimates $34.5 billion (Amazon and Epoch AI, 2026, T4-S8). The two figures have a different scope.

This value is inside the range of $30 million to $55 million for a site with chips. Thus the estimate of Epoch AI includes the chips.

Investor view

A site with power today is a scarce asset, but a small number of tenants stand behind the demand.

  • Value. Colocation is the rent of space and power in the data centre of a different owner. Vacancy in the main markets of North America was a record low of 1.4 per cent in the first half of 2026 (CBRE, 2026, T4-S26). One large owner expects a yield of 10.0 per cent or more on new sites (Digital Realty, 2026, T4-S27).
  • Risk. The tenants are a small number of AI companies. Oracle and OpenAI cancelled an expansion of 600 MW at Abilene in March 2026 (Tom's Hardware, 2026, T4-S11).
  • Forecasts. The forecasts of power demand differ by a factor of two or more. Do not use one forecast alone.
  • Cost. The chips are the largest line and energy is a small line. Thus the speed to get power matters more than the price of power.

Compute markets and the economics of one GPU

One GPU-hour has a cost and a price, and the useful life and the utilisation decide the margin between them.

In this module you learn who owns the GPUs, how to calculate the cost of one GPU-hour, and how companies finance the build.

The owners of the GPUs

Five hyperscalers own most AI compute, and the model companies rent it.

Five hyperscalers owned about 71 per cent of the AI compute of the world at the end of 2025. They are Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle. Their share was 63 per cent at the start of 2024 (Epoch AI, estimate, fourth quarter of 2025, T11-S19).

A GPU cloud is a cloud company that rents mainly GPUs for AI work. A second name is neocloud (Cisco, estimate, 2025, T19-S17). CoreWeave, Nebius and Lambda are examples. Epoch AI calls GPU clouds the most important owners after the hyperscalers, but it gives no share for them (Epoch AI, estimate, 2026, T11B-S8).

Worked example

CoreWeave had more than 1.7 GW of power in use in the third quarter of 2026. It had more than 4.6 GW of power under contract (CoreWeave, 10 November 2026, T11B-S10).

4.6 ÷ 1.7 = 2.7. Thus CoreWeave sold 2.7 times the capacity that it runs today. It must still build the rest.

Check A chart shows that Microsoft owns many GPUs. Does the chart show how much compute Microsoft uses for its own products?

No. Microsoft rents a part of its GPUs to customers such as model companies. The chart counts each chip at its owner.

Rental prices and the three types of contract

The same GPU has many prices, because the type of contract and the seller change the price.

  • On demand. The customer pays for each hour and makes no commitment.
  • Reserved. The customer commits to a fixed period and gets a lower price.
  • Spot. The customer buys spare capacity at a low price, and the seller can take it back at short notice.

A hyperscaler charges more than a marketplace. The customer also gets the other services of that cloud. Price indices can differ by up to 8 times in level. Thus always name the index and the date.

Worked example

A job needs 8 GPUs for 100 hours, which is 800 GPU-hours. On demand at AWS, the job costs 800 × $6.88 = $5,504.

On the spot market at about $2, the job costs about $1,600. But the seller can stop a spot job. Without a saved state, the customer loses the work.

Check A training job saves its state every 10 minutes and can start again. Which type of contract is suitable?

Spot. The job can accept a stop, thus it can use the lowest price.

The history of the H100 rental price

The rent of an H100 fell from 2023 to 2025 and rose again in 2026, but no single index covers the full period.

A third index measures contracts of one year. It had its low at $1.70 in October 2025, and it rose to $2.35 by March 2026 (SemiAnalysis, estimate, T5-S12).

The press connects the rise in 2026 to reasoning models and agents, which increased the demand for inference. Nvidia also stopped the production of the H100 (press, estimate, 2026, T5-S12).

Worked example

Index 2 was $2.00 on 9 December 2025 and $2.88 on 24 October 2026. $2.88 ÷ $2.00 = 1.44.

Thus the rent of a chip that was already old rose 44 per cent in less than one year. This fact supports a long useful life. Module 4 gave the opposite evidence from the sale prices of used chips.

Check An analyst joins the 2023 value of index 1 to the 2026 value of index 2 in one line. Why is this wrong?

The two indices measure different sellers with different methods. A difference in level can come from the method and not from the market.

Utilisation

A GPU costs money in each hour, but it earns money only in the hours with paid work.

Utilisation is the share of the hours in which a GPU does paid work. A GPU loses value each year, and the owner pays this cost for each hour of its life. The cost does not stop when the GPU has no work. Thus the owner wants paid work on the GPU for as many hours as possible.

McKinsey says that the returns of a GPU cloud stop their growth when utilisation is below 80 per cent (McKinsey, estimate, 2026, T5-S26). Normal companies are far below that level. One study of 23,000 company clusters found an average GPU utilisation of 5 per cent (Cast AI, estimate, 2026, T5-S25).

Simplified: the word utilisation has more than one meaning. A common measure shows only that the GPU was busy for a part of a period. A GPU can show almost 100 per cent and use one fifth of its capacity (FinOps Foundation, undated, T20-S9).

Check A company owns 8 GPUs. One GPU has work all the time, and seven have no work. What is the average utilisation?

12.5 per cent. One of eight GPUs works. The average hides the seven chips that earn nothing.

With a useful life of 3 years, it is $10,333 each year. Thus half the life gives two times the yearly cost. The cash does not change, because the company paid for the chip on the first day.

Check A company changes the useful life of its servers from 4 years to 6 years. What happens to its profit and to its cash?

The reported profit increases, because the yearly depreciation decreases. The cash does not change.

The cost of one GPU-hour

Three costs make one GPU-hour: depreciation, power and other running cost, and utilisation divides all three.

Calculate the cost of one paid GPU-hour in four steps:

  1. Divide the purchase price by the hours of the useful life. The result is the depreciation for each hour.
  2. Multiply the power of the GPU by the PUE and by the price of electricity.
  3. Add the other running cost for each hour.
  4. Divide the sum by the utilisation.

Worked example

Use an H100 as the example. The purchase price is $31,000 with its share of the server and the network (SemiAnalysis, estimate, 2024, old source, T16-S7). The useful life is 5 years, which is 43,800 hours.

Depreciation. $31,000 ÷ 43,800 hours = $0.708 for each hour.

Power. One eighth of a server of 10.2 kW is 1.275 kW. The PUE is 1.14 and the price is $0.0862 for each kWh. Thus the power costs 1.275 × 1.14 × $0.0862 = $0.125 for each hour.

Other cost. $0.30 for each hour. This is an example value for staff, maintenance and space.

Utilisation. The sum is $1.133. At a utilisation of 80 per cent, the cost is $1.133 ÷ 0.80 = $1.42 for each paid GPU-hour.

The market index for an H100 was $3.03 on 23 November 2026. The margin is ($3.03 − $1.42) ÷ $3.03 = 53 per cent.

Model companies and AI software businesses

An AI product pays for each use, thus its gross margin is lower than in traditional software, and its advantage is harder to keep.

In this module you learn to read the numbers of a model company and an application company, and to test a product.

The model company

A model company sells tokens at a high gross margin, but it spends more on training and research than it keeps.

The application company

An application company buys tokens and sells results, and its cost for each task decides its margin.

An application company pays a model company for tokens. It sells its product for a seat price, a usage price or a price for each result.

  • Price for each result. Intercom charges $0.99 for each customer case that its agent resolves. A case that a person handles costs $10 to $15 (Stripe and SaaStr, 2026, T8-S13).
  • Seat price with a usage limit. Cursor changed its $20 plan in June 2025 from a limit on requests to a limit on compute. Users received charges that they did not expect, and Cursor apologised (TechCrunch, July 2025, old source, T8B-S6).
  • Flat fee with credits. Salesforce used three price models for its agent product in 2025. It started with a price for each conversation. It then moved to a price for each action, and then to a flat fee with credits (SaaStr and Salesforce, 2025 to 2026, T8-S13, T8B-S7).

The cost for each task is the full model cost of one completed piece of work, with all its steps. Use is not equal across users. One example table gives a cost of $0.50 each month for a light user and $40 for a heavy user (Rework, estimate, 2026, T18-S8).

The result can be a negative margin. One report gave Cursor a gross margin of minus 23 per cent in the quarter to January 2026 (The Information, estimate, 2026, T8-S12). A later report put it near zero by April 2026 (Sacra, estimate, 2026, T11-S15).

Worked example

A support agent takes five steps for each customer case. One model of such an agent uses about 15,000 input tokens and 2,500 output tokens for each case. These token counts are an assumption of the source (analysis, estimate, 2026, T15-S15).

On GPT-6.2 Sol. 15,000 × $2 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.03. 2,500 × $10 ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.025. One case costs $0.055.

Only resolved cases earn money. Intercom quoted a rate of 67 per cent of cases resolved in March 2026 (Intercom, 2026, T15-S16). $0.055 ÷ 0.67 = $0.082 for each resolved case. Against a price of $0.99, the margin after model cost is 92 per cent.

On GPT-6.2 Astra. One case costs $0.22, and one resolved case costs $0.33. The margin after model cost is 67 per cent. Thus the choice of model changes the margin by 25 points.

Check A product has a seat price of $20. Its heaviest users cost $40 each month in tokens. Name two things that the company can do.

Give two of these actions. Set a usage limit. Add a usage price above the limit. Send simple work to a cheaper model. Increase the seat price for heavy users.

Why the gross margin is lower than in traditional software

Traditional software costs almost nothing for one more use, but each use of an AI product buys tokens.

ICONIQ finds that AI products had an average gross margin of 45 per cent in 2025. It projects 53 per cent for 2026 and 59 per cent for 2027 (ICONIQ, estimate, July 2026, T11-S26). Mature software usually shows 70 to 90 per cent (benchmark, estimate, 2026, T8-S11).

The model cost does not fall as an AI company grows. ICONIQ finds that inference is 23 per cent of revenue on average, at each size of company (ICONIQ, estimate, January 2026, T11B-S12).

Worked example

An application has a seat price of $30 each month. One user does 400 tasks each month. Each task uses 5,000 input tokens and 1,000 output tokens, at prices of $2 and $10.

One task costs $0.01 for input and $0.01 for output, which is $0.02. 400 tasks cost $8.00. The gross margin after model cost is ($30 − $8) ÷ $30 = 73 per cent.

A heavy user does 2,000 tasks. The model cost is $40, and the margin is minus 33 per cent. Thus the company loses $10 each month on this user.

The sources of an advantage that lasts

Five things can protect an AI product: private data, distribution, depth in a workflow, the cost to change supplier and own models.

The model alone is not an advantage, because each rival can buy the same model. One investor says that AI is only a way to deliver value to customers (a16z, 2026, T15-S1).

  1. Private data. The product has data that rivals cannot get, and each use adds to the data. But a platform can cut off data that it owns. Salesforce changed its terms in June 2025, and other companies can no longer store the messages of its Slack product (press, June 2025, old source, T15-S7).
  2. Distribution. The company already has the customers. More than 60 per cent of the new agent orders of Salesforce came from its present customers (Salesforce, 25 February 2026, T15-S6). But distribution is not a guarantee. Microsoft reached paid AI seats on about 3 to 7 per cent of its base (press, January and July 2026, T15-S5).
  3. Depth in a workflow. The product does the full job inside the systems of the customer. It writes results back, obeys access rights and keeps a record of each action.
  4. The cost to change supplier. The customer loses time or data when it leaves. For the model itself, this cost is low. Card data show that 43 per cent of the business customers of Anthropic came from a different supplier (Ramp, estimate, 2026, T15-S11).
  5. Own models. The company trains a small model for its own narrow task. Cursor built such a model on an open-weight model. It costs $0.50 for input and $1.50 for output for each million tokens (press, 2026, T15-S13).

The research found no measured study that connects private data or depth in a workflow to a better margin. The best measured signal is retention by price (research note, uncertain, November 2026, T15-S12).

Gross revenue retention is the share of recurring revenue that a company keeps from its present customers after one year, without upgrades. Net revenue retention also counts the upgrades (Stripe, read 26 November 2026, T19-S20).

Worked example

An AI product has 100 customers, and each pays $40 each month. The monthly revenue is $4,000. The product is in the group below $50, with a gross revenue retention of 23 per cent.

$4,000 × 0.23 = $920. Thus after one year only $920 of the monthly revenue remains from these customers. The company must find new customers for $3,080 only to stay at the same level.

Check A company reports a high net revenue retention and a low gross revenue retention. What does this pair of numbers show?

Many customers leave, and the customers that stay buy more. The upgrades hide the losses. Ask for the two numbers by price group.

Three tests for a strong product

Three questions separate a strong product from a thin layer on a model.

A thin layer is a product that adds only an interface to the model of a different company. The model company can add the same feature in one release. Investors use three tests (investor essays, estimate, 2026, T15-S1).

Two cases show the risk.

  • On 3 February 2026 Anthropic released a tool for legal work. The shares of Thomson Reuters fell about 15 to 18 per cent in one day. Analysts said that the tool did not do legal research, and the shares recovered in part (Morningstar, estimate, February 2026, T15-S8).
  • Chegg sold study help to students. It reported a fall in revenue of 36 per cent in one year, and it gave AI answers in search and chatbots as the cause (press, 2025, old source, T15-S10).

Worked example

Apply the better-model test to Chegg. Its revenue in the second quarter of 2025 was $105.1 million, after a fall of 36 per cent. $105.1 million ÷ 0.64 = $164.2 million one year before.

Thus the company lost about $59 million of revenue for each quarter. Better free models made its product less valuable. The product failed the test.

The product becomes less valuable when the model gets better, because the errors go away. It fails the test.

Investor view

Growth is real, but margin and retention separate the strong companies from the weak companies.

  • Value. Sierra raised money at a value of $15.8 billion in May 2026, and it reported recurring revenue of $400 million in October 2026. That is about 40 times revenue (TechCrunch and Sierra, 2026, T8-S6). Listed software companies trade near 4 to 5 times revenue (indices, estimate, 2026, T11-S33).
  • For model companies. The revenue of Anthropic grew about 47 per cent in one quarter. Its compute fell from 251 per cent of revenue to 48 per cent in five quarters (filing summaries, estimate, November 2026, T6-S1, T6-S17).
  • Against model companies. The commitments of Anthropic are about $895.4 billion, against revenue of $33.2 billion in nine months. The gross margin of 85 per cent excludes partner fees and free users.
  • Dependence. An application company depends on its model supplier. The supplier can increase its price, or it can release a rival feature.

Questions to ask

  • What is your gross margin with all compute included?
  • What is your cost for each task, and how does it change with volume?
  • What are your gross and net revenue retention for each price group?
  • Which model suppliers do you use, and what is your second source?
  • What happens to your product when the model company releases the same feature?

Builder view

An engineer protects the margin with measurement first, and then with cheaper models for simple work.

  • Measure. Record the model cost of each task with the customer and the feature. A cost for each seat hides the heavy users.
  • Keep a test set. A set of real customer tasks with correct answers is the first asset. Without it, the team cannot change the model safely.
  • Use cheaper models. Send simple work to a small model. Train an own model for a narrow task with high volume.
  • Test the lock-in. Measure the time to change the model behind one feature and to run the tests again. If it takes one day, the model is not the advantage.

What fails in practice. A platform cuts off the data that the product needs. A change of price surprises the users. A test project stops because the data access is poor, the owner is unclear or the cost at full size is a surprise.

Module 8

How engineers build AI applications, and what to use when

An AI application is normal web software with a model call inside, and four types of platform supply its parts.

In this module you learn the parts of an AI application, the method of work of a software team, and how to choose a platform.

The parts of a web application

A web application has seven parts, and a request from a user goes through them in a fixed order.

  • The front end is the part that runs on the device of the user and that the user sees.
  • The back end is the part on the server. It runs the logic and stores the data (glossary, estimate, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S17).
  • The database holds the data in tables. Postgres is a common type of database.
  • File storage holds files such as images and documents.
  • Sign-in checks who the user is.
  • The model API is the door to the language model.
  • The network edge is a group of servers near the users. A CDN (content delivery network) keeps copies of content on these servers, thus pages load faster (Cloudflare, 2026, T10-S2).

Worked example

How long does one answer take? Google rates the time to the first byte of a web page as good at 0.8 seconds or less (web.dev, 2026, T10-S3). One guide gives a time to first token below about 1 second for a chat product (ClickHouse, estimate, 2026, T11B-S19).

Then the model writes. An answer of 500 tokens at 71 tokens for each second takes about 7 seconds. Thus the model uses about 7 of the 9 seconds. The web parts are fast, and the model is the slow part.

Check Which part holds the data, and which part does the user see?

The database holds the data. The user sees the front end.

Retrieval and the vector database

Retrieval finds the relevant documents and puts them in the context, thus the model answers from the facts of the customer.

The full name is retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). It fetches relevant text from a data store and adds it to the prompt. Thus the model can use new data without new training (Cloudflare, read 26 November 2026, T19-S25).

A vector database is a database that stores embeddings and searches them (Cloudflare, read 26 November 2026, T19-S24). Many teams do not need a special product. Guides say that an extension of Postgres is sufficient for a store below about 5 to 10 million embeddings (guides, estimate, 2026, T1-S26).

Worked example

A company has contracts with 500,000 tokens in total. A user asks one question. Compare two designs at an input price of $2.

All contracts in the context. 500,000 × $2 ÷ 1,000,000 = $1.00 for the input of each question. Some suppliers also add a higher rate above 200,000 tokens.

Retrieval. The search returns 5 pieces of 800 tokens. These sizes are example values. With the question and the system prompt, the input is about 5,000 tokens, which costs $0.01. Thus retrieval is about 100 times cheaper for the input.

Retrieval decreases false statements, but it does not remove them. Module 2 gave the example of legal research tools with retrieval. For one document with a fixed size, a long context can be simpler than retrieval (engineer guides, estimate, 2026, T1B-S10).

Check In retrieval, which part does the search: the model or the application?

The application. It finds the pieces in the vector database and puts them in the prompt. The model only reads them.

Tools, the agent loop and memory

A tool lets the model act, a loop lets it work in steps, and memory is something that the application must build.

Tools. The model cannot run software. It writes a request to use a tool, the application runs the tool, and the application sends the result back (Anthropic, 2026, T1-S23). MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard that describes tools in one way for all applications (glossaries, uncertain, read 26 November 2026, T19-S27).

The agent loop. An agent repeats four steps until a stop rule ends the loop.

Memory. The model keeps nothing between requests. The application sends the history again, or a summary, or notes from a file (Anthropic, 2026, T10-S27). Context compaction replaces the old turns with a summary that the model writes (Anthropic, read 26 November 2026, T19-S12).

Worked example

A tracker counted 64,935 servers in the official list of MCP on 15 November 2026. One year before, the count was near 2,000 (tracker, estimate, November 2026, T1-S25).

64,935 ÷ 2,000 = 32. Thus the number of ready tools grew about 32 times in one year. A team can connect many systems without new code for each one.

Check An agent deletes a file. Which part did the deletion: the model or the application?

The application. The model only asked for the tool. Thus the team controls the risk through the access rights that it gives to each tool.

Evaluation, guardrails and monitoring

Three control parts show if the application is correct, safe and affordable.

  • An evaluation is a test that runs the application on fixed tasks and checks the outputs (OpenAI, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S1). A team runs it after each change of prompt or model.
  • A guardrail is a rule that limits what the application can say or do (NIST, read 26 November 2026, T19-S8).
  • Monitoring records each request: the model, the tokens, the cost, the delay and the tool calls (OpenTelemetry, 2026, T10-S29).

The main attack on an AI application is prompt injection. Prompt injection is an attack where text in the input makes the model ignore its instructions (OWASP, 2025, T19-S26). The text can hide in a document, a web page or an email that the model reads.

This attack has no complete fix. Anthropic tested one model in a browser. The attacks succeeded in 1.4 per cent of cases with safeguards, and Anthropic calls even 1 per cent a real risk (Anthropic, 24 November 2025, T10-S26).

One rule helps to find the dangerous products. An agent is most dangerous when it has three things together: private data, content from outside, and a way to send data out (Simon Willison, estimate, 16 June 2025, T10-S21).

Worked example

In a survey of about 1,300 people who build agents, 89 per cent use monitoring tools. Only 52 per cent run evaluations (LangChain, 2026, T1-S27).

89 − 52 = 37. Thus 37 of each 100 teams see what their agent does, but they do not test if it is correct. In the same survey, 32 per cent name quality as the main barrier.

Check A support agent reads customer emails, can see the customer database, and can send emails. Why is this design a risk?

It has the three things together: private data, content from outside and a way to send data out. A hidden instruction in one email can make it send private data.

The method of work of a team

A team changes software in small steps, and each step passes a review and automated tests before users see it.

  • A code repository is the store of all the code and its history.
  • A branch is a separate copy of the code for one change.
  • A pull request is a request to add a change to the main code. It invites a review by a second person.
  • Automated tests are programs that check the code after each change.
  • An environment is one running copy of the application. Staging is the copy for tests, and production is the copy for real users.
  • A release puts a change into production.

Tools enforce this method. GitHub can require a pull request, an approval and passed tests before a change reaches the main code (GitHub, 2026, T10-S4). A feature flag is a switch that turns a feature on for a small share of users. The team can switch a bad feature off without a new release (Martin Fowler, undated, T10B-S19).

AI coding agents. An AI coding agent writes code from an instruction. The developer gives the task, reviews the change and runs the tests. In one survey of 2026, 66 per cent of the developers who use AI work with coding agents (Stack Overflow, 6 October 2026, T1-S29). Google says that AI now writes about 75 per cent of its new code, and engineers review it (press, April 2026, T8-S23).

The evidence on speed does not agree.

  • Trials at three large companies found 26 per cent more completed tasks each week with an early AI tool (research draft, February 2025, old source, T8B-S12).
  • One trial with 16 experienced developers found that AI tools made them 19 per cent slower. They expected to be 24 per cent faster (METR, 10 July 2025, old source, T10-S34).
  • A large survey of 2025 found that AI makes a team more of what it already is. A good team gains, and a slow team gains little (DORA, 2025, T10-S9).

Worked example

A secret is a password or a key that gives access to a system. One security company checked public code. It found a secret in 3.2 per cent of the changes that one AI coding agent helped to write. The base rate was 1.5 per cent (GitGuardian, March 2026, T10-S25).

3.2 ÷ 1.5 = 2.1. Thus these changes leaked a secret about two times as often. The company warns that the tool is not the only cause. Fast code needs the same checks as slow code.

Check An AI agent writes most of the code of a team. Which step is now the limit on speed and quality?

The check step. The team must still review the changes, run the tests and scan for security faults.

Where the model sits

The application reaches the model directly at the model company, or through the service of a cloud.

  • Directly. The team uses the model API of the model company. The newest models arrive there first.
  • Through a cloud. The team uses the same model through a cloud service such as Amazon Bedrock. The cloud is then the processor of the data, and the cost is on the cloud bill (Anthropic, 2026, T12B-S5).

The token price can be the same on the two routes. Anthropic says that the prices of its models on Amazon Bedrock are equal to its direct prices on the global route. A route that keeps the work in one region costs 10 per cent more (Anthropic and AWS, 2026, T7B-S10, T2B-S10).

Check the data terms for each model. The newest models of Anthropic need a retention of data for 30 days. They are not available with zero retention unless Anthropic agrees (Anthropic, 2026, T12B-S3).

Worked example

A product sends 100 million input tokens each month at $2.00 for each million. On the global route the input costs $200.

On a route inside one region, the price is 10 per cent higher. The input then costs $220. Thus data location has a small and clear price here.

The route through a cloud service with a region setting. The team must also check the retention terms of the specific model.

How teams combine the four types, and what to use when

Many teams start on two managed platforms, and move single parts to a general cloud when a named limit arrives.

A managed platform is a platform that runs the servers for the team. A typical combination in 2026 has three parts. The front-end cloud hosts the web application. The back end as a service holds the database, the sign-in and the embeddings. The edge network supplies the CDN and the security. The team adds a general cloud when a large customer or a compliance rule needs it.

Limits of the evidence. No neutral survey confirms this pattern. One survey by Supabase asked about 2,000 builders. It gives Supabase 65 per cent, Vercel 45 per cent, Cloudflare 27 per cent and AWS 21 per cent. Supabase is a seller, thus read the result only as a direction (Supabase, estimate, June 2026, T17-S1).

Named companies moved in the two directions of the argument.

  • Monk, a start-up for financial data, moved its back end to AWS in August 2026. It wanted control over reliability, speed and the location of customer data (Monk, August 2026, T17-S3).
  • Loops made the same move with no stop of its service. It employed platform engineers before the move (Loops, 2026, T17-S4).
  • One writer argues that the move rarely pays at the size of a start-up, because the engineer time costs more than the saving (personal blog, estimate, 2026, T17-S5).

A start-up sells to hospitals. It has two product engineers, and it must sign an agreement on health data. Read the compliance row.

A front-end cloud and a back end as a service. The two types are strong on speed, and they need little set-up work.

How an application scales

The servers scale by copy, but the database must stay correct, thus the database is the difficult part.

A larger server or more servers. Vertical scaling means a larger server. Horizontal scaling means more servers that share the load (AWS, 2026, T10-S10). A load balancer spreads the requests across the servers.

More servers help only when a server keeps no data of its own. Then each server can answer each request, and the platform can add copies.

Serverless functions or servers that run all the time. A serverless function is code that runs only when a request arrives. The platform manages the servers and charges only for the time of use (AWS, read 26 November 2026, T19B-S18).

  • A serverless function costs nothing when it has no work, and it absorbs a peak. But it has a time limit. It can also have a cold start, which is the delay while the platform prepares a new copy (AWS, read 26 November 2026, T19-S23).
  • A server that runs all the time has no cold start and no time limit. But it costs money in each hour, and the team must manage it.

Why the database is difficult. The database holds all the data, and it must stay correct. Three methods help, and each has a limit (PostgreSQL guides, estimate, 2026, T10-S12).

  • A connection pool keeps a set of open connections that many clients share. It does not increase the maximum of the database.
  • A read replica is a copy of the database for reads only. It does not help with writes.
  • Sharding splits a database across servers. It scales writes, but it is hard to reverse.

Four more tools. A cache keeps a copy of a result for the next request. A queue holds slow jobs until a worker is free, thus the user does not wait. A rate limit protects the application from too many requests. A region is a group of data centres in one part of the world.

The three parts that fail first

When use increases, the database connections, the rate limit of the model API and the cost fail before the servers.

  1. Database connections. Each connection to Postgres is costly. The smallest server of Supabase allows 60 direct connections, and 200 clients through its pool (Supabase, 26 November 2026, T7-S4). Serverless functions open many short connections, thus they reach the limit quickly.
  2. The rate limit of the model API. The API returns the error 429. A limit on tokens for each minute often arrives before the limit on requests, because long prompts use many tokens (Anthropic, 2026, T18-S3).
  3. Cost. The usage charges grow with each request. One start-up saw bots send 66,500 requests in one day to a paid function. The team estimated a risk of $7,000 and stopped it in time (Metacast, February 2025, old source, T17-S8).

Worked example

The first tier of one model API allows 500 requests each minute. An agent task takes 10 steps, and each step is one request.

500 ÷ 10 = 50. Thus 50 agent tasks that start in the same minute fill the limit. Task number 51 gets the error 429 and must wait.

Reliability also has simple arithmetic. An availability of 99.9 per cent allows about 8.76 hours of stops each year. An availability of 99.99 per cent allows about 52.56 minutes (Google, 2026, T10-S15). Large platforms also stop. One region of AWS had a fault of more than 14 hours in October 2025 (AWS, October 2025, old source, T7B-S7).

The database connections, the rate limit of the model API, and the cost.

Investor view

A stack shows its risk in three places: the method of release, the parts that do not scale, and the usage charges.

  • Warning signs. No automated tests. No staging. One person who can release alone. Secrets in the repository. No tested restore from backup. No record of model calls.
  • Parts that scale with ease. Servers that keep no data, files on a CDN and serverless functions.
  • Parts that do not. The main database, a model API with a rate limit, and knowledge that only one person has.
  • Platform companies. The fixed plan is small, thus the revenue and the risk are in usage. Cloudflare reported revenue of $782.5 million in the third quarter of 2026, up 39 per cent in a year (Cloudflare, 5 November 2026, T7B-S3).
  • Dependence. A managed platform often runs on a general cloud. A fault of the cloud is then also a fault of the platform.

Questions to ask

  • How often do you release, and how often does a release fail?
  • Who can release alone, and who reviews AI-written code?
  • Where do you record the prompts and the outputs of the model?
  • Which permissions of your agents can write or delete data?
  • What is your model bill for each active user each month?
  • On which date did you last test a restore from backup?

Builder view

An engineer starts with managed platforms for speed, sets the limits before launch, and treats the model as a part that it cannot trust.

  • Start simple. Start on a front-end cloud and a back end as a service. Move one workload at a time when a named limit arrives.
  • Use the pool. Use the connection pool of the database from the first day.
  • Set a spend limit. Set a spend limit and rules against bots before the launch, not after the first large bill.
  • Queue long work. A model call that runs for minutes needs a queue. A stream of the answer does not extend the time limit of a function (Vercel, 26 November 2026, T17-S14).
  • Limit the agent. Give each agent the smallest set of permissions. Ask a person to approve an action that the team cannot reverse.
  • Separate the environments. Use different databases and keys for tests and for production.

What fails in practice. Row-level security is a database rule that decides which rows each user can read. One study of applications from an AI builder found that 170 of 1,645 exposed personal data through weak rules (security study, May 2025, old source, T10-S37). In two reported cases, an AI coding agent deleted a production database (The Register, July 2025, old source, T12B-S17) (press, estimate, 2026, T12B-S18).

Questions before a company buys AI

A buyer answers six questions before it signs: tasks, model size, price model, budget, value and data safety.

  1. Which tasks? Start with tasks that have a clear result that a person can check. One report says that bought tools succeed about two times as often as tools that a company builds (MIT, estimate, August 2025, old source, T1B-S8).
  2. Which model size? Use the smallest model that passes your own test. The input price of the largest models is 80 times the price of a small model.
  3. Seat or usage? A seat is good for many light users. A usage price is good for a small number of heavy users. Ask for a spend limit in the contract.
  4. How to budget? Multiply the tokens for each task by the tasks and by the price. Only one in five organisations forecasts its AI spend within 10 per cent (FinOps analysis, estimate, 2026, T12B-S9).
  5. How to measure value? Measure a result, such as cases that the tool completes. Do not measure only use. Suppliers claim resolution rates of 76 to 80 per cent, and one independent test measured 38 per cent (comparison sites, uncertain, 2026, T11B-S15).
  6. Is the data safe? Ask three things in the contract. Does the supplier train models on our data? How long does it keep our data? In which region does it process our data?

The large suppliers say that they do not train on the data of business customers by default (OpenAI, 2026, T12-S16) (Anthropic, June 2025, old source, T12-S17). But the terms differ for each model and each route. Ask for the terms of the specific model.

Staff also use tools that the company did not approve. IBM found that a data breach cost $670,000 more at companies with much unapproved AI use (IBM, July 2025, old source, T12-S26).

Worked example

A firm has 200 staff. Each person does 400 tasks each month at a model cost of $0.02 for each task. The usage cost is 200 × 400 × $0.02 = $1,600 each month.

A seat plan at $30 costs 200 × $30 = $6,000 each month. Thus usage is cheaper at this level. At 2,000 tasks for each person, usage costs $8,000, and the seat plan is cheaper.

Read three core KPIs of one GPU cloud together. The utilisation is high, the payback period is 22 months, and the useful life in the accounts is 5 years.

Four levels of skill

One course gives the map, and practice makes the expert.

The course uses four levels: aware, fluent, practitioner and expert. No published standard has these four names. The course built the ladder from three partial frameworks, thus do not present it as a standard (SFIA, UNESCO and Anthropic, 2024 to 2026, T18-S13, T18-S14, T13-S1).

The plan for 12 weeks

Twelve practical tasks, one for each week, move a reader with 3 to 4 hours each week from aware to practitioner.

The total time is 36 to 48 hours. Each task uses a part of this course.

Two findings help you to learn faster.

  • Ask the tool to explain its code. In one trial with 52 junior developers, the group with AI help scored 50 per cent in a test. The group without AI scored 67 per cent. The people who asked the AI for explanations kept more knowledge (Anthropic, 29 January 2026, T13-S34).
  • Set a budget before each build session. The plans of AI builders count credits or tokens, and a loop of failed corrections costs money.

Investor view

Evidence separates a strong company from a good presentation, thus ask for numbers that the company measured itself.

  • Value. AI is now its own part of the review of a company. In one report, one in five strategic buyers left a deal because of the effect of AI on the target (Hatchworks on Bain, estimate, 2026, T13B-S1).
  • Risk. Surveys of private equity firms do not agree on results. One finds that 14 per cent of chief executives report more revenue and lower cost together from AI (PwC, 2026, T8B-S20). Ask each company for measured profit.
  • Technical debt. Old code and missing tests change the price of a deal. They do not stop a deal. Ask for a list with the cost and the date of each repair.

Five questions when time is short

  • What is your model cost for each task, and what is its trend?
  • What is your gross margin with all compute included?
  • Show me the test results of your last three releases.
  • What do you do when your model supplier releases your main feature?
  • Which part fails first when your use doubles?

Builder view

An engineer prepares for a review with records, because records answer most of the twelve questions.

  • The repository. A reviewer reads the structure, the tests and the history of changes. One guide warns that this tour needs a plan and is not a full audit (Umbrex, 2026, T10B-S25).
  • The release record. It shows how often the team releases and how often a release fails.
  • The evaluation results. They show the quality for each release and each model.
  • The cost record. It shows the tokens and the cost for each customer and each feature.
  • The incident reports. A good report finds the causes and blames no person (Google, 2026, T10-S16).

What fails in practice. The team has no fixed test set. The costs have no label for the customer. The documents are old. One person holds the knowledge of a critical system.
